## The effect

- Landlords’ MRR increased from €700 to €50,000 per month
- A €4,500 decrease in monthly spending by cutting costs
  - Handling incoming requests without a customer support person: €3,000 → €0
  - Removing all forms of paid ads: €1,500 → €0
  - Contract-generating service costs reduced from €450 → €0

## The problem

Landlords always wanted more revenue. That meant finding tenants.

Since we had few bookings when I joined and they were mainly made by university students, I attended uni orientation sessions to learn more about their listing search process.

**That research uncovered a key detail:**
Students’ primary concern was finding accommodation quickly—far more important than their roommates, room size, or even, to a certain extent, location.

## The solution

Growing organically while weaning off paid services.

### Getting Friends in Flats in front of students beginning their search

Students coming to Vienna to study apply to every link on a university recommendation page.

To get Friends in Flats listed on 10+ university recommendation pages, I rode a city bike around town, developing relationships with university administrators.

Over time, this enabled us to generated more than all of our paid listing services combined.

### Optimizing the application funnel

When I first joined, I saw we weren’t tracking conversions correctly and set up PostHog to start monitoring events. I then ran tests and monitored results to increase application conversion rates.

Some key discoveries were:

- Identifying our flatmate-matching service was ineffective at converting tenants, so I built features that shifted the focus to pricing, listing details, and search tools.
- Spotting huge drop-offs at contract creation, which inspired a move from an expensive third-party contract generation service with rigid functionality to an in-house contract generator and signature tool I built.
- Setting up an automated test suite to help prevent downtime for new applications.

### Automating potential tenant enquiries

To manage incoming requests, I set up Intercom along with an MCP server, making the workload small enough for me to manage in a couple of hours a week instead of requiring a dedicated customer support person.
